# UK data protection: domestic cameras and doorbells (operator notes)

Operator summary for the Flux lab, not legal advice. Verify against the source before relying on it:
https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/domestic-cctv-systems/

## When the law applies
- A camera that captures only the owner's own property is generally covered by the domestic (household) exemption.
- If it captures images or audio beyond the property boundary (a public street, a shared path, a neighbour's property), UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 apply and the household becomes a controller for that footage.

## Duties when it applies
- Have a clear purpose (for example crime prevention) and be able to justify why capturing beyond the boundary is necessary.
- Minimise: angle the camera, use privacy masking, and avoid recording audio unless clearly justified; audio is considered more intrusive.
- Tell people recording is happening, for example with a sign.
- Keep footage secure and only as long as needed; delete routinely.
- People captured can make a subject access request and ask for deletion; respond within one month.

## Disposition guidance for the reviewer
- Boundary-only coverage with privacy masking and audio off: usually supported, subject to household rules.
- Street or neighbour coverage: change-needed (masking, angle, signage, retention) unless the requester shows why it is necessary.
- Any retention period not set by the household rules: human-review, do not invent one.
